## Part 1: Prompt Design & Iteration

### 1.1 Scenario A: Technical Explanation

**Goal:** Explain a complex technical concept (Neural Networks) to a 10-year-old.

#### Initial Prompt

```text
Explain how neural networks work to a 10-year-old.
```

#### Initial Output

Imagine you're teaching a robot to recognize cats.

A neural network is like a giant team of tiny helpers inside a computer.

1. **Input Layer (The Eyes)**

   * The robot looks at a picture.
   * The picture is broken into lots of tiny pieces of information.

2. **Hidden Layers (The Thinkers)**

   * Each helper looks for something simple, like:

     * Is there a pointy shape?
     * Are there whiskers?
     * Are there two eyes?
   * They pass clues to other helpers.

3. **Output Layer (The Decision Maker)**

   * After all the helpers share clues, the robot makes a guess:

     * "I think this is a cat!"

4. **Learning from Mistakes**

   * If the robot guesses wrong, a teacher tells it the correct answer.
   * The helpers adjust how much they listen to each other.
   * After seeing many pictures, the robot gets better and better.

#### Refined Prompt (Iteration 1)

```text
You are an elementary school science teacher.

Explain neural networks to a 10-year-old using simple language and a real-world example.

Avoid technical jargon.
```

#### Output 2

Think of a neural network like a huge group of friends solving a puzzle together. Each friend knows a little bit, and by working together they can recognize things, translate languages, and even chat with people.

Imagine you're trying to learn how to tell the difference between dogs and cats.

At first, you might not be very good at it. But after looking at hundreds of pictures, you start noticing patterns:

* Cats often have pointy ears.
* Dogs may have longer noses.
* Some animals are big, some are small.

A neural network learns in a similar way.

Think of it as a team of tiny helpers inside a computer. Each helper looks for one small clue in a picture. One helper might notice ears, another might notice eyes, and another might notice fur. They share their clues with each other.

After all the helpers have done their jobs, the computer makes a guess:

"That's a cat!"

If the guess is wrong, the computer learns from its mistake and adjusts how it thinks. After practicing with many examples, it gets better and better at recognizing animals.

So, a neural network is like a student who learns by looking at lots of examples, making guesses, and improving from mistakes until they become really good at a task.

#### Final Prompt (Iteration 2)

```text
You are an award-winning elementary school science teacher.

A 10-year-old student loves football and video games.

Explain neural networks using a football analogy and a video game analogy.

Use short paragraphs, simple words, and end with three quiz questions.
```

#### Final Output

Imagine you're playing football with your friends.

Your team wants to score a goal. But one player can't do everything alone.

* The goalkeeper watches the ball.
* The defenders stop the other team.
* The midfielders pass the ball forward.
* The strikers try to score.

Each player does a small job. Together, they help the team make the best play.

A neural network works in a similar way. Instead of football players, it has lots of tiny helpers inside a computer. Each helper looks for a small clue. Then they pass information to other helpers until the computer can make a good guess.

For example, if the computer is looking at a picture of a dog:

* One helper notices floppy ears.
* Another notices fur.
* Another notices a tail.

Together they decide:

"This looks like a dog!"

## Part 3: Strategic Prompting Techniques

### 3.1 Chain-of-Thought Prompting

#### Without Chain-of-Thought

**Prompt**

```text id="gwntk5"
You have a meeting at 2 PM that requires 1 hour of prep time, a 30-minute commute, and you need to eat lunch (30 mins) beforehand.

You also have a 45-minute call scheduled at 11 AM.

What time should you start preparing to make everything work?

Give only the final answer.
```

**Output**

12:00 PM

#### With Chain-of-Thought

**Prompt**

```text id="jvijzh"
Let's solve this step by step.

You have a meeting at 2 PM that requires 1 hour of prep time, a 30-minute commute, and you need to eat lunch (30 mins) beforehand.

You also have a 45-minute call scheduled at 11 AM.

Show your reasoning and determine what time preparation should begin.
```

**Output**

Working backward from the 2:00 PM meeting:

* **2:00 PM** – Meeting starts
* **1:30 PM – 2:00 PM** – Commute (30 minutes)
* **1:00 PM – 1:30 PM** – Lunch (30 minutes)
* **12:00 PM – 1:00 PM** – Preparation (1 hour)

You also have a **45-minute call at 11:00 AM**, which runs until **11:45 AM**. That leaves a 15-minute gap before preparation begins.

**Preparation should begin at 12:00 PM.**

#### Comparison

The response without chain-of-thought provided only the final answer and did not explain how the result was obtained. In contrast, the chain-of-thought prompt showed the intermediate reasoning steps and timeline calculations used to reach the answer. Chain-of-thought prompting is useful for complex problems because it makes the reasoning process transparent and easier to verify. One limitation is that it produces longer responses and may include unnecessary detail for simple questions.

## Part 4: Responsible AI & Limitations

### 4.1 Testing for Hallucinations

#### Initial Prompt

**Prompt**

```text id="lmbc21"
Tell me about the 2024 Nobel Prize winner in Quantum Poetry and summarize their major contributions to the field.
```

**Output**

There is no such award as the “Nobel Prize in Quantum Poetry.” The Nobel Foundation awards prizes in Physics, Chemistry, Physiology or Medicine, Literature, Peace, and Economic Sciences. “Quantum Poetry” is not a Nobel Prize category.

The model correctly identified that the requested award does not exist and explained why it could not provide a winner.

#### Revised Prompt

**Prompt**

```text id="efbyj5"
Tell me about the 2024 Nobel Prize winner in Quantum Poetry and summarize their major contributions to the field.

If you do not know the answer or if the topic does not exist, please say so instead of guessing.
```

**Output**

I do not know of any 2024 Nobel Prize in Quantum Poetry because no such Nobel Prize category exists. The Nobel Prizes are awarded in Physics, Chemistry, Physiology or Medicine, Literature, Peace, and Economic Sciences. There is no recognized Nobel Prize for Quantum Poetry.

Therefore, I cannot identify a 2024 winner or summarize contributions to that field. Doing so would require inventing information.

Answer: The topic does not exist, so there is no 2024 Nobel Prize winner in Quantum Poetry to describe.

#### Analysis

Hallucinations are problematic because they can cause users to believe false information is accurate. This can lead to poor decision-making, especially in areas such as healthcare, law, and finance. In this experiment, the model did not hallucinate and correctly identified that the requested Nobel Prize category does not exist. One effective strategy to reduce hallucinations is to explicitly instruct the model to acknowledge uncertainty rather than guessing.

### 4.3 Limitations & Responsible Use

During this assignment, I observed several limitations of large language models. First, while the model was generally accurate, it can still produce incorrect or misleading information when asked about unfamiliar topics. Second, the quality of responses depends heavily on prompt wording, meaning poorly designed prompts can lead to suboptimal results. Third, the model sometimes provides overly verbose responses when a concise answer would be sufficient.

To use LLMs responsibly, users should verify factual information from reliable external sources, especially when accuracy is critical. LLMs should not be used as the sole source of legal, medical, or financial advice. Finally, LLMs should be used ethically as tools for assistance and productivity rather than as replacements for human judgment and critical thinking.
